A new hire walks in on day one. No laptop, no documentation, no examples of past work, nothing about the customer. Just a sticky note: make it pop. Six months later the review says not a good fit.

No functioning team runs onboarding that way. It is, roughly, the standard operating procedure for AI tools inside most companies.

The model never saw the style guide. It never saw one example of what the team ships versus what it rejects. It does not know which numbers are sacred and which are placeholders. Underperformance is the wrong diagnosis. Under-informed is the right one, and no amount of prompt poetry fixes it.

The fix is the same as for the new hire, and it costs about ten minutes per recurring task: the goal, the constraints, one great example, one rejected example, the current source docs. The rejected example matters most. It draws the boundary that a model shown only good work cannot see.

Assemble the packet once. Reuse it every week. The output difference is visible the first day, and the packet outlives every prompt trick written this year.
