Context windows grew a hundredfold in three years, and somewhere along the way a useful discipline quietly died: deciding what actually belongs in front of the model.

Everything in a prompt competes for attention. The 40-page policy document pasted in because one paragraph might be relevant does not make the answer more grounded. It buries the paragraph that mattered under 39 pages of noise, and the bill goes up while the answer gets worse.

A habit worth installing for every recurring AI task. Write down three lists: what the model must know, what it must never see, and what changes per run. Retrieve the first. Exclude the second, which includes half-finished internal notes and anything confidential that was pasted in for convenience. Template the third.

Then test the trimmed version against the kitchen-sink version on real cases. The trimmed one wins more often than intuition predicts, and it is faster and cheaper every single time.

Attention is the scarce resource. Tokens were only ever the meter.
