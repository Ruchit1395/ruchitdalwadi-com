The edict came down from on high: every product manager had to ship an AI feature by Q3. The team responsible for the internal meeting scheduler, the one with the whiteboard covered in sticky notes, got their assignment.

They spent the next six months building an AI to read an email thread and automatically book a meeting for everyone on it. This was a huge project. It required a new database, three separate microservices, and a custom trained model.

After a massive push, they launched. And it worked. The system correctly booked the meeting about 95 percent of the time.

The only problem was that it replaced a task their executive assistant, Dave, was already doing. Dave was already on payroll and his accuracy was closer to 99 percent. The company spent a fortune to build a feature that saved them almost nothing and performed slightly worse than a person.
