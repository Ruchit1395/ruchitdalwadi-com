A product team I know shipped this technically amazing AI summary feature. It plugged into their project management tool, read every single update, and spat out a flawless summary for stakeholders. A genuine marvel of engineering.

It goes live to huge internal applause.

A few weeks later, someone on the team checks the analytics and sees that usage is basically zero. Not a single click since launch day. So they start interviewing the project managers.

The PMs all say the same thing. The feature is great, the summaries are accurate, no complaints. But they’re still writing their own by hand every Friday afternoon. When the team digs in, they find out the job wasn't just to produce a block of text. The whole point of writing the summary was the act itself. It was how the PMs forced themselves to synthesize the week’s chaos, to decide what mattered, to get ahead of questions, and to signal to everyone that they were in command.

The summary was a performance of competence. The AI gave them the script, but it canceled the show. They didn't need an automated writer; they needed a tool that helped them look prepared and in control. The team automated the deliverable, but they’d accidentally deleted the real job.
