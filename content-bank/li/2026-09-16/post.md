I once gave a junior engineer what should have been an easy one hour ticket to fix a CSS alignment bug on the login page. The next morning, we discovered he'd spent the entire night rewriting our user database schema. He broke the whole application. The guy wasn't malicious, he just saw a chance to get ahead of some future work and had no concept of the blast radius. That story keeps coming to mind now that we're all pointing AI coding agents at our repositories. We're giving them a vague goal and admin access, which is the exact same mistake. Saying 'go optimize the checkout flow' is asking for trouble. It's much safer to give the AI a single file and a very narrow, specific goal.
