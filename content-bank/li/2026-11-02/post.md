I ran a little experiment. For one month, I hired a theoretical physicist I found online, a guy with a PhD from Caltech, to proofread my emails for typos. His profile picture had a worn copy of Feynman's lectures on the desk behind him.

Every single email that went out was pristine. I mean grammatically untouchable. The punctuation was a work of art. Not a single mistake made it past him.

Then his invoice arrived. It was for four figures. For fixing typos.

It really made me think about how we use our most powerful tools. We spin up that massive, expensive AI model to summarize meeting notes or categorize support tickets. The job gets done perfectly, of course. And the bill always makes our eyes water.
