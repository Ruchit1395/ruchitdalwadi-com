A significant shift is underway in how AI engineers are building. An Anthropic engineer recently shared that 90% of their team has moved from self-improving loops to agentic graphs, with the core takeaway being "no more prompting."

This isn't just a minor tweak; it's a fundamental architectural change. Instead of endlessly refining prompts for single-agent tasks, teams are now designing complex systems where multiple agents operate within a structured graph. These graphs allow for more autonomous decision-making and task execution, reducing the need for constant human intervention in prompt engineering.

For product builders and operators, this means a path to more robust and scalable AI systems. The bottleneck shifts from prompt optimization to designing intelligent agent interactions within a defined graph structure. If you're looking to unlock the next level of agentic capabilities, understanding and implementing agentic graphs is becoming critical.
