# Becoming AI-Native Campaign

Purpose: document what AI-native work means for an individual and for companies at different stages, using a real transformation journey as the source of insight without exposing private organizations, people, customers, systems, or data.

## Campaign thesis

Adding AI to existing tools does not make a person or company AI-native. The change happens when goals, context, decisions, actions, review, and learning become one connected work loop.

The series follows the 36 lessons in the original user-authored playbook:

1. The decision to own the context layer.
2. The platform foundations that make the work connect.
3. The AI harness: structure, context, gates, identity, and reliability.
4. The software factory that turns evidence into verified delivery.
5. The process and culture needed for trustworthy autonomy.
6. Honest measurement and the closing operating thesis.

`APPROVED_TOPIC_SEQUENCE.md` is the semantic lock. The bank and queue must follow it exactly. A validator failure blocks publication.

## Content standard

Every post must include:

- one clear claim;
- one concrete example, approved fact, or clearly labeled hypothetical;
- one useful test, step, or decision;
- one honest limitation, problem, or tradeoff.

The hub is a source of operating insight, not a product to promote. Public posts may discuss generalized capabilities such as goals, shared context, proposals, approvals, action history, memory, and integrations. They may not reveal private implementation details.

## Evidence policy

`PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md` controls personal claims, achievements, KPIs, timelines, named tools, replacement claims, and failure stories.

- `approved`: may be used as written.
- `generalize`: teach the lesson without the identifying detail.
- `needs approval`: do not publish the claim or number.
- `prohibited`: never publish.

No automation may turn a private repository fact into a public receipt merely because it appears in a local file.

## Cadence

- X: at most one native post per IST day.
- LinkedIn: at most one native post per IST day.
- Both platforms use the same numbered lesson, with different hooks, structure, examples, and endings.
- X should be compact and pointed. LinkedIn should explain the operating implication with more room, not simply expand the same sentences.
