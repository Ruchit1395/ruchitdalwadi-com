# Performance Playbook

This file controls how core lessons become native posts. It does not permit hype, bait, invented receipts, or adjacent engagement actions.

## The new rule

Do not publish a lesson merely because it is correct. Publish it when it contains tension, proof, consequence, and a useful mechanism.

## Post anatomy

Every draft needs four beats, but their order should vary:

- `Tension`: a failure, misconception, cost, conflict, or surprising contrast.
- `Proof`: an approved personal receipt, visible artifact, exact mechanism, or labeled hypothetical.
- `Meaning`: what this teaches about becoming AI-native.
- `Use`: a diagnostic, action, decision, or boundary the reader can apply.

Do not label these beats in the post.

## The 25K LinkedIn reference pattern

The verified 25K-impression LinkedIn outlier is analyzed in `LINKEDIN_25K_POST_PATTERN.md`. Its reusable strength was not a generic storytelling formula. It made an abstract AI problem visible through a familiar workplace scene, then delivered a sharp reframe and an exact fix.

Use this sequence when the lesson supports it:

`recognizable scene -> AI-native pivot -> wrong diagnosis -> better diagnosis -> exact fix -> human close`

Rules:

- At least two of every three LinkedIn posts must open with an approved real scene, an observable workflow, or a clearly labeled hypothetical that a reader can picture.
- Put concrete nouns and actions before framework labels. Show the source document, approval, copied result, rejected output, open tabs, or correction before naming the operating model.
- Build around one repeatable contrast. More than one slogan weakens the post.
- Give three to five exact inputs, checks, or actions when the lesson offers a repair.
- A light human ending is welcome when natural. Never force a joke.
- Do not copy the reference post's new-hire analogy, wording, labels, or punch line.

## Hook rules

- The first line must be understandable without the rest of the post.
- Prefer 6 to 14 words on LinkedIn and fewer than 90 characters on X.
- Ban openings shaped like `Here are`, `A useful way`, `Your first`, `In today's`, or a dictionary definition.
- A question is allowed only when it exposes a real diagnostic. It may not be empty engagement bait.
- Do not open two consecutive posts with the same family.

Hook families:

1. Confession: `I built several AI workflows before admitting most were not AI-native.`
2. Failure: `The automation worked. The company workflow still got worse.`
3. Contrarian claim: `Adding AI to every tool is not an AI-native strategy.`
4. Before and after: `The first version drafted work. The later version moved work.`
5. Artifact: `This six-part loop is the architecture I wish I had started with.`
6. Diagnostic: `If AI disappears and only the writing gets slower, the workflow was never AI-native.`
7. Named tension: `Replacing Jira is the wrong goal. Replacing avoidable handoffs is better.`

## Receipt mix

Across any five consecutive lessons:

- at least two use an approved personal build receipt;
- at least one uses a concrete failure or limitation;
- at least one uses a framework, architecture, or diagnostic;
- no more than one uses a hypothetical as its main proof.

Approved evidence IDs must be recorded in the draft check. A principle without proof may still be used, but not in two consecutive posts.

## Platform difference

### X

- Aim for 350 to 650 characters unless the idea truly needs more.
- Use one sharp contrast, one receipt, and one memorable test.
- Remove background that does not change the conclusion.
- Prefer a closing line that can stand alone.

### LinkedIn

- Aim for 650 to 1,050 characters.
- Put the human or company tension in the first two lines.
- Add one layer X does not have: a short scene, company-stage comparison, build decision, or failure consequence.
- In scene-led posts, delay the abstract term until the reader can see the problem.
- Change the opening, paragraph order, and closing. Do not translate the X post into longer prose.

## Ending rotation

Rotate among:

- a diagnostic rule;
- a boundary;
- a small action;
- an unresolved tension;
- a specific observation readers can compare with their work.

Do not end every post with `Pick one`, `Try this`, or `The tradeoff is`.

## Pre-publish performance gate

Reject the draft unless all are true:

1. The first two lines create a reason to continue.
2. The post contains one concrete noun or mechanism unique to this journey.
3. The proof is approved or clearly hypothetical.
4. The reader can repeat the central contrast in one sentence.
5. The platform version differs structurally from its counterpart.
6. The ending is not the same shape as the previous two posts.
7. The draft does not promise engagement or use bait to request it.
8. The opening gives the reader a scene, object, action, or consequence to picture before it starts teaching.
