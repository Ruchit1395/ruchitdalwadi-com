# LinkedIn 25K Post Pattern

Checked live on 2026-08-14 IST.

## Verified reference

- Public post: https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7481650828890787840/
- Visible performance: 25K impressions and 49 engagements.
- The next two visible posts in the 365-day analytics view had 762 impressions with 5 engagements and 668 impressions with 1 engagement.
- Account-level context for the same 365-day view: 32,130 impressions, 19,205 members reached, and 91 percent of impressions from outside the network. The out-of-network figure is account-level, not proof of this post's audience mix.

This is a strong outlier and a useful creative reference. It does not prove that one format caused the distribution.

## Why the post was easy to enter

The post did not open with an AI definition. It opened inside a familiar workplace failure. A reader could picture the laptop, missing documents, absent examples, vague instruction, and unfair performance review before AI was mentioned.

The structure then moved cleanly:

1. A vivid human scene.
2. A surprising pivot from the scene to company AI use.
3. A sharp diagnosis that moved blame from the model to the setup.
4. Concrete missing inputs rather than abstract advice.
5. A short onboarding checklist.
6. A light final line that made the lesson memorable.

The language used objects and actions, not category labels. The central contrast was easy to repeat. The fix could be tried immediately.

## Pattern to reuse

Use this sequence when the lesson supports it:

`recognizable scene -> AI-native pivot -> wrong diagnosis -> better diagnosis -> exact fix -> human close`

The scene may come from a real approved receipt or a clearly labeled hypothetical. It must explain the operating problem, not decorate it.

For this campaign:

- Start with a moment a person can see: a draft waiting for context, an approval trapped in chat, a correction that never reaches the next run, or five tools open for one outcome.
- Put concrete nouns and verbs before framework language.
- Name one misconception and replace it with one memorable distinction.
- Give three to five exact inputs, checks, or actions.
- Close with a diagnostic, consequence, or restrained human observation.

## What not to copy

- Do not reuse the new-hire analogy, its wording, its punch line, or its central label.
- Do not force humor into serious topics.
- Do not turn every post into the same six-beat template.
- Do not invent a scene, number, timeline, failure, or achievement.
- Do not confuse high impressions with guaranteed engagement or future reach.

## AI-03 application

The reset post now begins with the observable gap in the author's early builds: a fast answer followed by manual source-finding, judgment, transfer, approval, and correction work. Its repeatable contrast is:

`I had automated the typing, not the work.`

That keeps the lesson original while applying the strongest feature of the reference post: show the work problem before naming the framework.
