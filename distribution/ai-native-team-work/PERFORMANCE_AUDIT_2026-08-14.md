The sample is small, but the result is unambiguously weak. AI-02 did not earn a visible interaction on either platform.

## The 25K LinkedIn outlier

The 365-day LinkedIn analytics view revealed a much stronger reference post: 25K impressions and 49 engagements. Its public URL is https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7481650828890787840/.

The same view showed 32,130 total account impressions, 19,205 members reached, and 91 percent of impressions coming from outside the network. That audience split is account-level, not post-level. Still, the reference post clearly dominated the visible annual results. The next two visible posts had 762 impressions with 5 engagements and 668 impressions with 1 engagement.

The outlier differed from the weak campaign posts in five important ways:

1. It opened with a workplace scene anyone could picture.
2. The AI lesson arrived through a surprising but clear analogy.
3. Concrete objects and missing inputs carried the argument.
4. One sharp contrast made the diagnosis repeatable.
5. The repair was an exact checklist, followed by a light human ending.

This is a creative signal, not causal proof. The campaign will reuse the mechanics while avoiding the post's new-hire analogy, wording, labels, and punch line. The full analysis is in `LINKEDIN_25K_POST_PATTERN.md`.

## Secondary comparator

A recent LinkedIn post outside this campaign opened with a real missed-schedule failure, explained the hidden tracking flaw, and gave a three-field repair. It reached 155 impressions, 3 reactions, and 2 comments.

That post had roughly 4.7 times the impressions of AI-02. It also created discussion. It differed in four ways:

1. It began with something that actually went wrong.
2. The author was inside the story.
3. It showed a specific mechanism, not a general principle.
4. The repair was concrete enough to copy.

This is a directional content signal, not proof that one format will always win.

## Why AI-01 and AI-02 underperformed

### Abstract hooks

`Your first AI workflow should fit on one page` and `AI projects often fail at the handoff` are reasonable lessons, but they create little curiosity, tension, or consequence.

### No reason to believe this author

The posts removed every personal receipt. They sounded like advice assembled from best practices rather than lessons earned while building systems.

### Repeated schoolbook structure

Both posts used the same rhythm: claim, instructions, tradeoff, assignment. That made the campaign feel templated after only two days.

### Weak platform difference

LinkedIn received a longer explanation of the same lesson. It did not receive a stronger story, professional tension, or stage-specific example.

### Audience mismatch

The profiles promise practical help for people starting with AI, while the campaign must eventually cover company architecture and operating-model change. Each advanced idea therefore needs a simple entry point and a concrete human problem.

### Distribution ceiling

Organic reach is currently small. Better copy cannot guarantee large distribution. The campaign must improve both the percentage of viewers who react and the chance that the platform expands distribution. Native-post duty will improve content and measurement; comments and relationship-building remain separate workflows.

## Immediate correction

AI-03 becomes a candid reset built around an observable workflow failure: the AI answer appeared quickly, but source-finding, judgment, transfer, approval, and correction still happened manually. It uses the approved build journey as proof and defines AI-native work with one repeatable contrast: `I had automated the typing, not the work.`

The next sequence alternates:

1. personal reset;
2. contrarian misconception;
3. clear comparison;
4. architecture model;
5. diagnostic test;
6. named tool tension.

This prevents six consecutive lecture posts.

## Measurement plan

Record each post at 2 hours and 24 hours.

- X: views, likes, replies, reposts, profile visits when visible.
- LinkedIn: impressions, reactions, comments, reposts, profile viewers when attributable.
- Calculate visible interaction rate as visible interactions divided by reach.
- Compare each post with the trailing five native posts on the same platform.
- Do not change the thesis after one post. Change a hook or format after two comparable misses; change a chapter after four.

## Near-term success criteria

These are learning thresholds, not promised outcomes:

- At least one substantive comment or reply across the next three paired lessons.
- At least one of the next three posts exceeds the trailing-five platform median reach by 50 percent.
- At least two of the next three lessons include an approved personal receipt or concrete build artifact.
- No two consecutive posts use the same hook family or ending shape.
