# Privacy and Language Rules

These rules are mandatory for every public post in this campaign. They override the source note and any draft.

## Never reveal

- A company, product, domain, customer, employee, team, founder, or partner name.
- Team size, reporting lines, roles tied to a real person, hiring state, or who performed an action.
- Private customer requests, quotes, tickets, calls, messages, documents, or commercial terms.
- Database structure, schemas, table or column names, record shapes, identifiers, keys, queries, row counts, or screenshots.
- Exact internal stack, vendor combination, account setup, credentials, endpoints, repositories, file paths, security controls, or infrastructure layout.
- Private costs, revenue, usage, performance numbers, model spend, incident dates, or internal success metrics.
- A real incident, migration, failure, or result in a way that could identify the company or people involved.

If a useful point depends on any private detail, replace it with a general rule, a clearly labeled hypothetical example, or a stated tradeoff. If that removes the value, skip the topic.

## Language rules

- Use simple human language, one useful point, short sentences, and everyday words.
- No em dashes or en dashes.
- No hype, AI slop, filler, generic inspiration, engagement bait, hashtags, emoji, or links in the body.
- Do not use `game-changer`, `revolutionary`, `insane`, `unlock`, `leverage`, `seamless`, `paradigm`, `thought leadership`, or `the future is here`.
- Do not say or imply `we built`, `our team`, `our company`, `our customers`, or `after a year`.
- Do not invent a personal experience, customer story, result, metric, or quote.
- Technical terms are allowed only when needed and must be explained in plain words on first use.
- Humor may target broken process, never a person.
- End with a concrete action, boundary, test, checklist, or tradeoff. Do not end with a generic question.

## Platform rules

- X: 350 to 900 characters by default, 3 to 6 short paragraphs.
- LinkedIn: 600 to 1,200 characters by default, 4 to 7 short paragraphs.
- X and LinkedIn may teach the same numbered lesson on the same day, but must not be verbatim. Change the hook, structure, example, and closing action.
- No thread, carousel, image, poll, file, or body link unless the user explicitly asks.
