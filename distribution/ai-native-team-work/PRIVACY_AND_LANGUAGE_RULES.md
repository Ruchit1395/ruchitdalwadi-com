# Privacy, Evidence, and Language Rules

These rules override source notes and drafts.

## Never reveal

- Private organization, product, domain, customer, employee, team, founder, or partner identities.
- Team size, reporting lines, hiring state, or roles tied to an identifiable person unless explicitly approved.
- Private requests, quotes, tickets, calls, messages, documents, or commercial terms.
- Database structure, record shapes, identifiers, keys, queries, row counts, screenshots, or private data samples.
- Exact internal stack, vendor combination, account setup, credentials, endpoints, repositories, file paths, security controls, or infrastructure layout.
- Private costs, revenue, usage, performance, model spend, incident dates, or success metrics.
- Customer-impacting incidents, security issues, or failures that could identify the company or people.

## Evidence rules

- `PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md` is the publication authority for personal receipts.
- A fact in a local repository is private unless the ledger approves it.
- Never invent a personal experience, result, metric, timeline, quote, or replacement claim.
- “Replaced” means the old tool or function is no longer used in the stated scope. Integration, reduced use, or duplication removal does not prove replacement.
- KPI claims require a baseline, new value, measurement window, review cost, and scope.
- When evidence is unapproved, teach the method, use a clearly labeled hypothetical, or omit the claim.

## Language rules

- Use simple human language, short sentences, and one useful point.
- No em dashes or en dashes.
- No hype, AI slop, filler, generic inspiration, engagement bait, hashtags, emoji, or body links.
- Do not use `game-changer`, `revolutionary`, `insane`, `unlock`, `leverage`, `seamless`, `paradigm`, `thought leadership`, or `the future is here`.
- Technical terms are allowed only when needed and explained in ordinary words.
- Humor may target broken process, never a person.
- End with an action, boundary, test, checklist, or tradeoff.

## Platform rules

- X: 350 to 900 characters by default, in 3 to 6 short paragraphs.
- LinkedIn: 600 to 1,200 characters by default, in 4 to 7 short paragraphs.
- The platforms may teach the same lesson but must differ in hook, order, example treatment, and closing.
- No thread, carousel, image, poll, file, or body link unless explicitly requested.
